# Results

First results from session S (Phase 5): 96 training runs at commit `4a4a467`, each evaluated in MJX
with 256 paired episodes per test setting. Everything here is regenerated by

```bash
uv run python scripts/analyze.py        # runs/_analysis/: per_setting.parquet, per_run.csv, conditions.csv, hypotheses.json
uv run python scripts/make_figures.py   # docs/figures/*.png
```

Rules and thresholds are those of the frozen [pre-registration](preregistration.md). Success threshold
τ = 0.1552 m/s, computed from condition A alone before any other condition was analysed. Numbers are IQM over
seeds with 95 % stratified-bootstrap CIs (5,000 resamples) unless stated. Not yet included: sim-to-sim
transfer (native MuJoCo), dynamics perturbations, probing (H6), gait analysis.

## Main finding: under randomized delay, most MLP policies never learn to walk

Final returns are bimodal (Fig. 2). A seed either learns to walk (return ≈ 26–31) or settles into
standing still without falling (≈ 19–21: tracking error ≈ 0.79 m/s, the size of the command; success ≈ 2 %;
falls ≈ 1–2 %). Seeds that walk after 200M steps:

| A | B20 | B40 | B60 | **C** | **D** | E | **F** | C_obs | D_obs | E_obs | C_tm | D_tm | E_tm | DH2 | DH8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5/5 | 5/5 | 2/5 | 1/5 | **0/10** | **0/10** | 7/10 | **0/10** | 4/5 | 1/5 | 5/5 | 2/5 | 0/5 | 1/5 | 0/3 | 0/3 |

Standing runs reach the standing plateau by ≈ 20M steps and stay there to 200M. Runs that escape it under
delay do so late: E between 60M and 180M steps; B40 and B60 at 80M–160M. A escapes by ≈ 40M. Knowing the
true latency (F) does not help; the recurrent policy (E) does. This is a property of the pre-registered
training setup (one PPO configuration for all conditions, 200M steps), not of the evaluation. Training
returns show the same plateau, and the latency sampling and delay mechanics were checked. Per the
pre-registration, these runs are reported as they are and not rerun with other settings.

Consequence for the hypotheses: H2–H4 were meant to compare robustness and nominal cost among policies that
walk. Their rules come out "not supported", but the reason is that C and D did not learn the task, not a
robustness trade-off.

Where the policies walk, the two channels are close to interchangeable. The one point outside ±10 pp is
E_obs at 100 ms, at −10.8 pp, and its CI overlaps the margin. Half of the six policies are at the floor, so
the rule's "at most half floor/ceiling points" condition fails for them, which makes the verdict
inconclusive.

**H6 detail** (`scripts/probe.py`, D42; ridge probe of the per-episode action delay, 128 trace episodes with
delay U{0..80 ms}, 5-fold episode-level CV; `runs/_analysis/h6.json`, `probe_per_run.csv`):

| Ridge R² by seed (`lat` set) | s0 | s1 | s2 | s3 | s4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E, GRU state | 0.73 | 0.21 | 0.29 | 0.73 | 0.79 |
| E, random GRU (same inputs) | 0.42 | 0.01 | −0.03 | 0.48 | 0.49 |
| E walks? (final return) | yes | no | no | yes | yes |
| D, last hidden layer | −0.04 | −0.02 | −0.07 | −0.07 | −0.09 |
| A, last hidden layer (survivors only) | 0.40 | 0.46 | 0.42 | 0.44 | 0.40 |
| F (privileged latency input; positive control) | 0.74 | 0.81 | 0.79 | 0.83 | 0.71 |

- The rule fails on D, and clearly (CI of R²(D) − R²(A) is [−0.52, −0.44]). D never walks (F43): a robot
  standing still produces no delay signature, and a random network fed D's inputs also scores ≈ 0. So this
  says nothing about whether a *walking* history policy would encode latency.
- E's GRU state carries latency information, much of it already recoverable from a random GRU on the same
  inputs (0.42–0.49 in walking seeds). In every seed the trained state beats the random GRU on the same inputs, by
  0.20–0.32 (paired, exploratory; the pre-registered comparison is unpaired, and with E's walk/stand split
  across seeds its CIs are too wide to decide). Walking seeds reach R² ≈ 0.75.
- A's 0.42 is on survivors only (39 % of steps alive; A falls at ≥ 40 ms), so its target range is narrower
  (SD 3.2 vs 5.9 substeps). It is a weak control for this reason.
- Adding dynamics randomization (`lat_dyn`) lowers R² by ≤ 0.05 everywhere (E 0.56, A 0.38). The prior
  report of near-zero encoding with five randomized factors (arXiv 2603.21268) used a different setup, so
  this is not a direct replication attempt; here the GRU's R² stays well above zero.
- The MLP probe (secondary, fixed settings) overfits where there is no signal (R² ≈ −0.6 for C/D) and adds
  nothing where there is (E 0.55, A 0.10); F 0.93. Not used for any conclusion.

Bimodal conditions make IQMs and CIs wide, because they mix walking and standing seeds. Per-seed values are
in `runs/_analysis/per_run.csv`. E's seven walking seeds have L50 of 14–83 ms; its three standing seeds have 0.
Actor parameter counts are within ±3 % of A's 192,408 (E 188,792; D 194,296; F 193,432).

Other observations, descriptive only:
- A (trained at 0 ms) collapses between one and 1.5 control steps of delay. Success is 89 % at 12 ms, 49 % at
  20 ms and 7 % at 28 ms, and 99.7 % of episodes fall at 40 ms. Its observation-delay L50 is the same, 20 ms.
- The fixed-delay specialist B20 keeps ≈ 90 % success from 0 to 20 ms and fails by 60 ms. It is the most
  robust condition in which every seed walks.
- Policies trained on observation delay (C_obs, E_obs) learned far more reliably (4/5 and 5/5) than those
  trained on action delay (C 0/10, E 7/10). Their walking seeds tolerate ≈ 85–110 ms on both channels.

## Sim-to-sim (native MuJoCo) and dynamics perturbations (exploratory)

All 90 finished runs were replayed in native MuJoCo (C engine, float64; the MJX runs use JAX, float32) on 64
episodes per cell, paired with the MJX grid's episodes (`scripts/eval_native.py`; parity tests in
`tests/test_native_parity.py`).

- **Transfer is near-exact.** Per-cell native − MJX success: mean −0.1 pp, SD 1.2 pp, largest 7.8 pp
  (1,530 cells). Per-run L50: mean difference −0.14 ms, largest 4 ms (E seed 7: 36 vs 40 ms); whether L50 is
  censored agrees in 90/90 runs. This covers test-time action delay and the perturbations below. Observation
  delay, jitter and motor lag were not replayed natively, and it says nothing about whether the training
  failure is simulator-specific (all training was in MJX).
- **Delay and dynamics interact for the baseline.** At 0 ms A is insensitive to every perturbation tested
  (92–97 % success). At 20 ms (A's own L50) it is fragile: success 47 % nominal, 19 % on half friction,
  23 % with motors 1.2× stronger. Stronger motors hurt because higher PD gain raises the loop gain, so the
  same delay leaves less phase margin; weaker motors (0.8×) leave A at 54 %.
- **The delay-trained policies are not.** B20 (trained at 20 ms) and E change by ≤ 5.2 pp and ≤ 3.6 pp between 0 and 20 ms
  under every perturbation; they keep the usual mild sensitivity to weak motors (0.8×: B20 74–78 %,
  E 44–47 %, vs. 88–90 % and 61–64 % nominal) and low friction.

| Native success (IQM over seeds) | nominal | friction 0.5× | payload 3 kg | motor 0.8× | motor 1.2× |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, 0 ms | 96 % | 93 % | 93 % | 92 % | 97 % |
| A, 20 ms | 47 % | 19 % | 42 % | 54 % | 23 % |
| B20, 20 ms | 90 % | 85 % | 83 % | 78 % | 94 % |
| E, 20 ms | 61 % | 49 % | 53 % | 44 % | 65 % |

E's numbers include its 3 standing seeds (≈ 2 % each), which bound its IQM.

## Robustness over training (exploratory)

- **The baseline gets more latency-fragile as it keeps training.** A's success at 0 ms rises to 91 % by 60M
  steps and keeps creeping up (96 % at 200M). At 20 ms its IQM is 67 % at 60M and 56 % at 200M; at 40 ms it
  peaks at 10 % (40M) and reaches 0 %. The start point was picked after seeing the curve. Per-seed linear
  slopes from 60M on are −17, −12 and −7 pp per 100M steps in three seeds and ≈ flat (+0.7, −2.4) in two, with
  32 episodes per point (≈ ±8 pp). Suggestive only.
- **The delay-trained policies do not.** B20's success at 20 and 40 ms rises and plateaus (90 % and ≈ 65 %).
- **E's IQM was still rising at 200M steps** (20 ms: 52 → 64 % over the last two checkpoints), but mostly
  because of late-escaping seeds: 2 of the 7 walking seeds gained ≈ 25 pp, the other 5 gained 0–9 pp, and the 3
  standing seeds never left ≈ 3 %. A longer budget might help E's late seeds. Nothing here suggests it would
  help the MLPs: C is at ≈ 3 % in every seed at every checkpoint.

## Status of this version and next steps

Closed as a first version (2026-11-08): the pre-registered verdicts are reported as they are, with the learning
failure under randomized delay as the main result. Not run on any other PPO recipe, so whether the failure is
specific to this recipe, budget or simulator is open. The project's original question (which mitigation buys the
most robustness per unit of training cost) cannot be answered from these runs: C, D and F never walk, leaving only
the GRU.

Deferred: a longer-budget or latency-curriculum follow-up for all conditions, gait analysis, time-resolved probes,
delay vs. motor-lag cross-evaluation, videos, a literature search on the learning failure, and a paper.
